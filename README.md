037019 Multicenter Study

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript introduces ECTRS, a Qwen2.5-7B model fine-tuned via LoRA to generate diagnostic impressions from free-text ear CT findings. The central claim is that specialty-specific fine-tuning on a large, multicenter Chinese-language dataset substantially outperforms general-purpose LLMs on this task, evaluated across 118,907 report pairs from 11 hospitals using automated metrics and blinded radiologist review.
Two concerns dominate. The core approach — LoRA fine-tuning of a foundation model on domain-specific report pairs — is now well-established, and the manuscript does not articulate a methodological advance over LLM-RadSum (Zheng et al., Radiology 2025) or RadAdapt (Van Veen et al., 2023). The contribution is subspecialty application, not method. The evaluation infrastructure is also structurally limited: two radiologists reviewed 1,000 cases from within the same institutional ecosystem as the training data, and no prospective or clinical utility signal is presented.

2. Strengths
The scale and multicenter design are the clearest strengths. 118,907 report pairs from 11 hospitals, with a genuine held-out external test set from seven institutions not involved in training, exceeds most comparable studies in the field. The blinded human evaluation incorporated stratified sampling across age, sex, and findings-text length, with four independently defined scoring dimensions and inter-observer agreement quantified via Cohen's weighted kappa (κ = 0.72–0.77). The multivariable analysis identifying findings-text length as a significant predictor of poor LCS F1 (OR 0.092 for ECTRS, p < 0.001) is clinically interpretable and directly actionable for future data augmentation.

3. Weaknesses
The methodological contribution is thin. LoRA fine-tuning of a 7B-parameter model on structured clinical text pairs is a solved engineering problem, and ECTRS's advantage over comparators is attributable to training distribution alignment, not architectural innovation. The human evaluation is conducted by only two radiologists with general CT experience — not subspecialty otologic temporal bone expertise — making factual error detection in rare entities such as otosclerosis and fibrous dysplasia unreliable. The comparator models were applied without prompt optimisation, rendering the performance gap uninterpretable as genuine architectural superiority. Finally, the study is entirely Chinese-language and retrospective, with no prospective deployment data, making the clinical utility claims in the conclusion speculative.

4. Editorial Decision
Reject. The method is standard LoRA fine-tuning with no novel component; the core finding that specialty-adapted LLMs outperform general-purpose models in narrowly defined NLP tasks is already established by LLM-RadSum and RadAdapt. The comparator evaluation is confounded by absent prompt optimisation, the human evaluation is underpowered in both reviewer count and subspecialty expertise, and no clinical utility signal is presented. The authors should be directed toward Radiology: Artificial Intelligence or European Radiology, where well-executed subspecialty applied validations are appropriately scoped.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Reviewers should cover the following areas: (1) LoRA-based fine-tuning of autoregressive language models for clinical text generation, with experience in Chinese-language biomedical NLP; (2) automatic evaluation metrics for natural language generation — specifically BLEU, BERTScore, and LCS in the clinical summarisation context, including their known failure modes; (3) human evaluation methodology in AI-assisted radiology, including inter-rater reliability design and rubric construction; (4) otolaryngology radiology, specifically temporal bone and ear CT interpretation, at subspecialty level; (5) clinical workflow integration of AI documentation tools, with awareness of regulatory and medicolegal implications in the Chinese or comparable healthcare context.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review (Past 3 Years)
The radiology impression generation field has moved rapidly since 2023. Van Veen et al. (Nature Medicine, 2024) demonstrated that domain-adapted LLMs can match or exceed medical experts in clinical text summarisation across multiple specialties, establishing LoRA-style adaptation as the field baseline. Zheng et al. (Radiology, 2025) published LLM-RadSum — the most directly comparable work — showing a fine-tuned 7B model achieved a median LCS F1 of 0.75 on internal testing and 0.44 externally against GPT-4o (F1 0.30), with radiologist-level acceptability in over 81.5% of outputs across diverse anatomical regions and modalities. The design of LLM-RadSum is structurally near-identical to ECTRS: same parameter scale, same LoRA adaptation strategy, same internal/external split logic. Xu et al. (Radiology, 2024) demonstrated a WiNGPT-based 7B model fine-tuned on 800 radiology reports, with GPT-4 as evaluator. RadCouncil (Zeng et al., 2024) extended the paradigm to multi-agent architectures for chest X-ray impression generation, improving diagnostic accuracy and stylistic concordance over single-agent approaches.
Against this landscape, ECTRS occupies a narrow niche: it is the first application in ear CT specifically, which is a legitimate novelty claim. However, the narrow anatomical focus and the absence of a methodological advance mean the manuscript largely replicates the LLM-RadSum design within a different subspecialty and language context. The manuscript does not engage with RadCouncil, does not benchmark against RadAdapt, and does not situate ECTRS against Van Veen et al.'s broader claim about adapted LLMs and expert performance. These omissions weaken the introduction and the discussion's contribution framing.

038400 clinical data

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript proposes GaussDiff, a generative framework coupling Box-Cox-based Gaussian alignment with a TabDDPM-style denoising diffusion probabilistic model to address class imbalance in clinical tabular data. The central claim is that aligning marginal distributions of clinical variables with the Gaussian prior recovers minority-class structure that resampling and generic deep generative methods miss, improving sensitivity for low-incidence events without sacrificing overall discrimination. The problem is real and the framing technically coherent.
The key concern is novelty. The core contribution — applying a power transformation before tabular diffusion — is incremental relative to TabSyn (ICLR 2024), TabDiff (ICLR 2025), and Imb-FinDiff, which address distribution mismatch and class imbalance in tabular diffusion from directly overlapping angles. Neither is cited nor benchmarked. A secondary concern is that the downstream classifier is a random forest with default hyperparameters across all tasks, conflating data augmentation quality with a single arbitrary classifier's behavior.

2. Strengths
The multi-cohort evaluation spanning SHDS (n = 2,982), a pregnancy cohort (n = 404), CHARLS (n = 8,054), and NHANES (n = 4,917) constitutes meaningful external validation absent in most synthetic augmentation papers. Consistent sensitivity gains under the TSTR protocol across all four cohorts, tested via Welch's t-test across 15 independent runs, substantially reduces the risk of a chance finding.
The ablation design cleanly isolates the contribution of Gaussian alignment and global modeling through GaussDiff (unaligned) and GaussDiff (local), providing mechanistic support for design choices rather than empirical assertion alone.
The privacy evaluation incorporates membership inference attacks under three attacker models with attacker AUCs near random-guessing baseline across all cohorts — more rigorous than DCR-only analysis standard in this literature.

3. Weaknesses
The technical contribution is insufficiently differentiated. TabSyn normalizes marginal distributions via a VAE latent space before diffusion; TabDiff introduces feature-wise learnable noise schedules — a strictly more general solution to the same mismatch GaussDiff addresses via fixed Box-Cox preprocessing. The authors neither benchmark against these methods nor argue why a fixed transformation is preferable to learned alternatives. This is a scientific gap, not a writing issue.
Sensitivity is reported under a fixed 0.5 random-forest threshold throughout. Performance differences may partly reflect classifier-specific fit rather than intrinsic data quality. Results with at least one additional classifier would substantially strengthen the causal claim.
Model calibration is absent. For a tool intended for clinical risk stratification, Brier scores and reliability diagrams stratified by cohort are standard requirements under TRIPOD guidelines. Their omission materially weakens the clinical translation claim.

4. Editorial Decision
Decision: Reject — invite resubmission after major revision. The multi-cohort evaluation and ablation design are genuine strengths, but the unresolved novelty gap relative to TabSyn and TabDiff cannot be addressed editorially. Reviewers should adjudicate: (1) whether the Box-Cox alignment contribution is non-redundant with learned feature-wise schedules; (2) whether sensitivity gains are robust across classifiers; and (3) whether calibration reporting meets standards for the claimed clinical application.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Reviewers should include specialists in: denoising diffusion probabilistic models for mixed-type tabular data, with specific familiarity with TabDDPM, TabSyn, and TabDiff architectures; synthetic data evaluation methodology including membership inference attack frameworks and privacy-utility trade-off analysis; clinical prediction model methodology, including TRIPOD reporting standards, calibration assessment, and survival analysis in imbalanced cohorts; epidemiology of cardiovascular risk in diabetes, with experience in CHARLS or NHANES dataset analysis; and obstetric outcomes in gestational diabetes, particularly composite adverse pregnancy outcome prediction.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review (Past 3 Years)
The field of diffusion-based tabular data synthesis has advanced rapidly since TabDDPM (Kotelnikov et al., ICML 2023), which established the baseline this manuscript builds upon. TabSyn (Ma et al., ICLR 2024) improved upon TabDDPM by learning a VAE latent space before diffusion, achieving better fidelity on mixed-type distributions — addressing essentially the same marginal mismatch problem GaussDiff targets, through a learned rather than fixed transformation. TabDiff (Shi et al., ICLR 2025) introduced feature-wise learnable noise schedules in a unified continuous-time diffusion framework, offering a principled generalization of the fixed Gaussian schedule that GaussDiff patches via preprocessing. Imb-FinDiff (ArXiv 2023) explicitly addressed class-imbalance in tabular diffusion for financial data, the direct analog of GaussDiff's primary use case. In healthcare-specific synthetic tabular generation, EHRDiff (TMLR 2024) and FlexGen-EHR (ICLR 2024) demonstrated EHR-tailored diffusion synthesis with heterogeneous variable types. On the clinical prediction side, the emerging paradigm of retrieval-augmented tabular in-context learning (TabPFN, LocalPFN) offers inference-time adaptation without retraining that sidesteps the class imbalance problem differently. GaussDiff occupies a legitimate niche — clinical deployment with modest sample sizes and interpretability requirements — but must position itself explicitly against TabSyn and TabDiff, whose published results on mixed-type tabular benchmarks overlap directly with this paper's experimental domain.

038309 microbiome analysis"

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript introduces "crowd augmentation" — replacing human citizen-science contributors with behaviorally-cloned FCN agents within the Borderlands Science pipeline — to produce BLSA, an automated system generating a de novo phylogeny from 953,000 16S rRNA V4 sequences. Downstream signal extraction uses AHLD2, a memory-efficient reimplementation of the Adaptive Haar-like Distance applied to ~20,000 AGP subjects.
The work does not meet the Nature Communications bar. The phylogenetic improvement over BLS is statistically undemonstrated and numerically marginal — the compound metric in Table 1 favors BLS (58.16) over BLSA (48.81). Biological signal recovery cannot be causally attributed to crowd augmentation rather than AHLD2 parameterization or sequence clustering choices. These are structural deficiencies, not revision-addressable ones.

2. Strengths
The crowd augmentation formalism is well-posed. Replacing the human solver kernel with a stochastic kernel trained under bounded divergence while preserving the consensus mechanism is conceptually rigorous. The nondeterministic inference policy — gap count sampled uniformly ±2 from base value — is a principled mechanism for maintaining solution heterogeneity without which consensus degenerates to a deterministic pass-through.
The AHLD2 scalability contribution is technically sound. Eliminating the explicit dictionary matrix A (~640 terabytes for the AGP dataset) via Rayleigh quotient reformulation, and replacing SMACOF with randomized classical MDS to reduce memory from O(n²) to O(nr), is a genuine advance enabling the population-scale analyses described.

3. Weaknesses
Table 1 presents no confidence intervals, no p-values, and no permutation tests. The compound metric regresses from BLS (58.16) to BLSA (48.81). KC vs. GG2 differs by 23 units with no variance estimate. The manuscript cannot claim BLSA outperforms BLS without establishing that any difference exceeds sampling noise.
The causal attribution of signal improvement to alignment quality is unestablished. BLSA, BLS, and GG2 differ simultaneously in alignment quality, coverage distribution, topology, and branch-length calibration. No ablation holds the phylogenetic tree fixed while varying alignment. Improved signal recovery under BLSA may be driven entirely by AHLD2's KTA-based hyperparameter optimization over a 6,000-sample stratified subsample rather than upstream alignment.
The "super-healthy" filter analysis (Figure 4D) is underreported. The size of the resulting cohort is not stated, its demographic composition is not characterized, and excluding ~20 conditions simultaneously — including allergies and lactose intolerance — may inflate classification performance through selection rather than signal quality.

4. Editorial Decision
Reject. The central performance claim is contradicted by the compound metric in Table 1, and downstream biological signal recovery cannot be attributed to crowd augmentation without a structural ablation that is absent from the current design. Transfer recommendation follows below.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Reviewers should cover: phylogenetic inference from short amplicon MSAs, specifically de novo construction with FastTree and evaluation against Greengenes2-scale references using KC and Triplet distance; behavioral cloning and stochastic inference policies for citizen science automation; the Adaptive Haar-like Distance framework and random-forest-based beta-diversity metric learning at population scale; large-scale 16S V4 microbiome cohort epidemiology with experience in AGP or TMI data structures; and gut microbiome dysbiosis in IBD with familiarity with the RISK cohort taxonomy literature.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review
Sarrazin-Gendron et al. (Nature Biotechnology, 2024) — the direct BLS predecessor — established that gamified citizen science improves 16S phylogenetic inference and UniFrac effect sizes over PASTA, MAFFT, and MUSCLE across 946,740 AGP sequences. The present manuscript's marginal phylogenetic gains over that baseline are indistinguishable from noise absent statistical testing. The original AHLD (Gorman & Lladser, PLOS Computational Biology, 2024) introduced the Haar-like wavelet basis and random forest proximity reformulation; AHLD2's SMACOF-to-randomized-MDS swap is computational re-engineering of that framework, and the manuscript does not clearly delineate what is mathematically novel beyond engineering. The field has also seen progress in phylogenetic placement approaches — EPA-ng, SEPP — and alignment-free phylogenetic inference for 16S data, competing directions the manuscript does not engage. The absence of comparison against EPA-ng or SEPP as baselines is a gap the BLS paper itself highlighted, which this manuscript inherits without resolution.

7. Suggested Reviewer Names
Phylogenetic inference / MSA evaluation: Alexandros Stamatakis (Heidelberg Institute for Theoretical Studies), Frédéric Lemoine (Institut Pasteur), David Posada (Universidade de Vigo).
Beta-diversity / AHLD / statistical microbiomics: Levi Waldron (CUNY Graduate School of Public Health), Elhanan Borenstein (Allen Institute / Tel Aviv University), Jesse Shapiro (McGill University).
IBD microbiome epidemiology: Rinse Weersma (University of Groningen), Subra Kugathasan (Emory University), Eran Segal (Weizmann Institute).

Transfer Recommendation
npj Biofilms and Microbiomes is the strongest fit if the biological signal recovery and microbiome-phenotype association results are considered the primary contribution. It publishes population-scale microbiome analyses with computational methodological components and has the readership best positioned to adjudicate the AHLD2 signal recovery claims and cross-cohort validation.
Communications Biology is the better fit if crowd augmentation and the AHLD2 algorithmic advance are the primary contribution. It tolerates a higher methods-to-biology ratio and has published comparable computational biology frameworks without requiring the clinical translation depth that Nature Communications expects.
npj Systems Biology and Applications is a plausible tertiary option if the authors reframe crowd augmentation as a general paradigm for human-AI hybrid systems in biological data analysis, de-emphasizing microbiome-specific signal recovery claims.
Regardless of destination, the statistical gap in Table 1 and the ablation deficit must be addressed before any venue will accept the performance claims as written.

0375516 Tiered Hospitals

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript presents CHD-CARE, a two-stage spatiotemporal transformer framework for four-class pediatric CHD echocardiographic diagnosis (Normal, VSD, ASD, PDA) from color Doppler video. The central claim is that collaborative AI — combining probability-based diagnostic assistance with structured visual guidance via key-frame selection and defect-region localization — can narrow expertise- and tier-related performance gaps in tiered healthcare systems. The randomized three-phase reader study is a substantive methodological investment that elevates this above routine benchmarking.
The primary concern is scope restriction. The model is evaluated exclusively on isolated, single-subtype acyanotic lesions — the simplest end of the pediatric CHD spectrum — which materially limits the translational claim embedded in the title.

2. Strengths
The multicenter cohort spans eight centers across national, provincial, municipal, and county tiers with heterogeneous acquisition hardware (Philips EPIQ, GE Vivid, Mindray Resona). Seven prospectively assembled, fully held-out external validation cohorts yielding external one-vs-rest AUROCs of 0.950–0.984 support generalizability more credibly than internal cross-validation.
The three-phase crossover reader study uses Latin-square pseudo-randomization, two-week washout periods, and stratification by both tier and seniority. Quantifying decision-revision quality via NRI (0.078, 95% CI 0.066–0.089) and IDI (0.092, 95% CI 0.083–0.101) is methodologically appropriate and uncommon in AI reader studies.
The visual guidance design is grounded: residual deviation from a frozen view-specific normal baseline drives key-frame selection, and a blinded acceptability assessment anchors the IoU = 0.25 threshold to reader-rated clinical utility rather than geometric precision alone. Decomposing reader behavior into appropriate reliance, under-reliance, and over-reliance — and demonstrating that visual guidance reduced under-reliance from 13.4% to 7.1% — provides behavioral insight beyond aggregate accuracy.

3. Weaknesses
The restriction to isolated VSD, ASD, and PDA excludes concomitant defects and complex phenotypes that dominate real-world pediatric CHD practice. The claim of "reliable pediatric CHD diagnosis across tiered hospitals" is overstated relative to this scope. The authors should quantify the exclusion rate for concomitant subtypes from the screened 4,968 subjects.
The consensus reference standard excludes cases with expert disagreement — precisely the most diagnostically ambiguous cases where AI support is most needed. This systematically inflates reported performance, and the exclusion rate due to label disagreement should be disclosed.
Prevalence-adjusted calibration is absent. Cohort positive rates (670:776 internal, 216:419 external) diverge substantially from real-world screening prevalence. Without calibration plots and expected calibration error, the reported class probabilities cannot be trusted for deployment guidance.

4. Editorial Decision
Send for External Review, with mandatory major revision conditions. The prospective multicenter cohort, three-phase reader study, and reliance calibration analysis together represent evaluation rigor that warrants peer review. Reviewers should specifically adjudicate: whether the clinical scope framing is adequately bounded; whether reference standard exclusion inflates primary performance estimates; and whether the prevalence mismatch is addressed through calibration analysis.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Reviewers should include expertise in the following areas: spatiotemporal video transformer architectures and attention-based interpretability methods for medical video classification; multi-view fusion and incomplete-data robustness in echocardiographic deep learning; randomized crossover human-AI interaction study design with specific attention to reliance calibration and NRI/IDI methodology; pediatric echocardiography and CHD diagnosis in tiered or resource-limited healthcare settings; and clinical AI evaluation methodology with emphasis on calibration, prevalence adjustment, and real-world deployment readiness.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review (Past 3 Years)
The echocardiography AI landscape has advanced rapidly in the past three years, with the field moving from task-specific classification models toward foundation and vision-language architectures. Christensen et al. (Nat. Med. 2024) developed EchoCLIP, a vision-language foundation model trained on echocardiogram-report pairs that generalized across a broad range of cardiac conditions. Vukadinovic et al. (Nature 2025/2026) subsequently demonstrated EchoPrime, a multi-video view-informed model trained on 12 million video-report pairs with comprehensive study-level prediction capabilities. Holste et al. (JAMA 2025) presented PanEcho, a multitask ConvNeXt model trained on over one million echocardiogram videos addressing 39 clinical outputs simultaneously. In the pediatric CHD domain specifically, Jiang et al. (WJPS 2023) reported a seven-view CNN achieving AUC of 0.91 on 1,411 cases from a single Chinese pediatric center, and Cheng et al. (Research 2024) validated a multi-view multi-modal approach for CHD detection from transthoracic echocardiograms. CHD-CARE is clearly differentiated from this literature in two respects: its focus on color Doppler video (rather than grayscale cine loops) for shunt detection, and its explicit human-AI collaboration framing supported by a prospective reader study. The paper does not engage meaningfully with PanEcho or EchoPrime, both of which represent directly relevant multi-view fusion baselines and should be discussed. The manuscript also does not address whether its two-stage ResNet-50-plus-transformer architecture provides advantages over pre-trained foundation encoders as backbones — an important question given the computational cost of training from scratch on 2,081 subjects.

037727 Cardiac Conditions

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript introduces PPG-CLIP, a cross-modal contrastive framework aligning a PPG transformer encoder with patient-level EHR summaries to produce clinically grounded waveform representations. The central claim — that embedding patient-level clinical context during pretraining yields more transferable representations than signal-level self-supervision — is evaluated across four downstream tasks with a reported average relative improvement of 26.7%. The ablation infrastructure is more thorough than most submissions in this space.
The work does not clear the Nature Communications bar. The pretraining cohort is a single emergency department. The blood pressure result does not approach clinical acceptability thresholds. Interpretability claims rest on post-hoc embedding correlation. Critically, a plausible EHR-to-downstream diagnostic label leakage pathway in the AF detection task is neither acknowledged nor audited.

2. Strengths
Dataset construction is methodologically careful. Temporal causal filtering restricts EHR entries to those recorded before the PPG timestamp, and patient-level separation between pretraining and all five downstream sets is explicitly enforced. The 721,840-segment corpus from 3,618 unique patients is large by existing PPG pretraining standards.
The perturbation strategy ablation in Table 7 is the most technically informative result. Latent dropout substantially outperforms waveform-level augmentations — cutout AF F1: 0.72 versus latent dropout: 0.88 — providing direct empirical support for the core design argument that waveform augmentations corrupt diagnostically relevant morphological features.

3. Weaknesses
The EHR summarisation pipeline carries an unaudited diagnostic label leakage risk that undermines the AF detection result. MedCPT embeds ICD-10 diagnostic codes into the patient-level clinical summary. Pre-existing AF-related codes in any patient with documented AF history would be encoded and contrastively aligned with the PPG encoder. The reported F1 of 0.883 may therefore reflect label leakage rather than generalisation from waveform features. This requires explicit code-level exclusion analysis before the result is credible.
The blood pressure result is presented without clinical contextualisation. PPG-CLIP achieves MAE = 8.04 mmHg versus SiamQuality's 8.60 mmHg — a 0.56 mmHg absolute reduction. The AAMI SP10 standard requires MAE ≤ 5 mmHg for clinical acceptability. No method in Table 3 approaches this threshold, and the authors do not mention AAMI. Reporting a 6.5% relative improvement without disclosing that all systems remain substantially below the clinical deployment standard is a material misrepresentation.
The interpretability analysis in Figure 2 is circular. Pearson correlation between PPG embeddings and EHR token embeddings measures training-objective alignment, not clinical interpretability. The token atrial fibrillation correlating at r = 0.85 with the PPG representation is the expected output of a contrastive objective explicitly trained to align these modalities.

4. Editorial Decision
Reject, with transfer recommendation to npj Digital Medicine. The unaudited EHR label leakage pathway in the AF detection task is a fundamental validity concern requiring experimental re-analysis, not revision. The absence of demographic subgroup analyses for a system claiming clinical scalability, and the failure to contextualise BP results against AAMI standards, reflect clinical validation gaps incompatible with this journal's expectations. The latent dropout finding, PSUP mechanism, and ablation design are above average for the field and are likely suitable for npj Digital Medicine, where single-centre methodological contributions with thorough ablation meet publication standards.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Should the transfer proceed to peer review, reviewers should cover: transformer-based self-supervised pretraining for physiological time series, with specific experience in cross-modal contrastive training dynamics and temperature sensitivity; clinical text encoder design for EHR data, particularly retrieval-oriented pretraining objectives such as MedCPT and their alignment properties; PPG waveform morphology and beat-level signal processing, including systolic phase characterisation and artefact classification; non-invasive blood pressure estimation and familiarity with AAMI SP10 and IEEE 1708 validation standards; and emergency or hospital medicine with experience in EHR data governance, diagnostic code semantics, and the clinical implications of patient-level context for arrhythmia detection.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review
The PPG foundation model field has moved rapidly. PaPaGei (Pillai et al., npj Digital Medicine, 2024) established morphology-aware and participant-aware contrastive pretraining evaluated across 20+ tasks. Pulse-PPG (Saha et al., IMWUT, 2025) demonstrated that field-trained models on uncurated real-world PPG outperform clinically pretrained counterparts on generalisation benchmarks, directly challenging the implicit assumption in PPG-CLIP that high-quality ED data is a superior pretraining substrate. AnyPPG (Nie et al., 2025) uses co-registered ECG as a cross-modal supervision signal on over 100,000 hours of recordings — achieving strong multi-task performance without requiring EHR access at all, which partially undermines PPG-CLIP's claim that EHR grounding is uniquely necessary. A concurrent submission leveraging ICU co-registered ECG and respiratory signals as contrastive selectors reports 36–42% improvements over prior art with 3× fewer pretraining subjects, raising the efficiency bar further. Apple Research's participant-level contrastive selection work similarly outperforms augmentation-based frameworks at scale.
PPG-CLIP's conceptual niche — using structured longitudinal EHR text rather than co-registered signals as the cross-modal anchor — is genuinely distinct from all of the above, and the respiratory rate gain is the strongest empirical argument for its value. However, the authors do not engage with AnyPPG or the ECG-supervised foundation model literature, omissions that are conspicuous given their direct relevance. The manuscript's framing as a scalability contribution is also weakened by the fact that Pulse-PPG (28.5M parameters, open-sourced) already outperforms 200M–385M general-purpose time-series models on wearable tasks, repositioning parameter efficiency as table stakes rather than a distinctive claim.

037112 language models

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript introduces LENS (Layer-wise Evidence-based Neural Suppression), a framework for selective unlearning of patient-identifying information from clinical LLMs. The central claim is that identity recall concentrates within specific transformer layers, and that LoRA-based suppression confined to a causally localized window (L4–7) achieves distributional alignment with a reference model while preserving medical reasoning performance. The clinical motivation — post-deployment data withdrawal under GDPR-equivalent mandates — is legitimate. The most serious concern is novelty: mechanistic unlearning via activation patching and layer-localized intervention has been substantively published at ICML 2024 and submitted to ICLR 2025, and LENS does not adequately differentiate itself from this directly competing work.

2. Strengths
The activation patching analysis on medgemma-4b-it reveals a two-stage identity recall profile — early binding at L1–4 and amplification at L16–22 — that is specific to clinical narrative text and distinguishes this work from general factual recall localisation literature.
The dual-metric evaluation using ROUGE-L and Truth Ratio (TR) is analytically stronger than most unlearning benchmarks. It correctly surfaces distributional failures in refusal-based methods that output-level metrics would miss.
The scaling experiment across deletion ratios of 5–40% meaningfully demonstrates LENS's stability advantage over global update methods, which is a practically relevant finding absent from most comparable evaluations.

3. Weaknesses
The core novelty is insufficiently differentiated from Pochinkov & Schoots (arXiv:2410.12949, ICLR 2025) and Hernandez et al. (ICML 2024), both of which apply causal tracing to mechanistically localize and suppress factual recall. Clinical domain adaptation alone does not constitute a primary contribution at this level.
The paper includes no adversarial robustness evaluation — no paraphrase probing, no membership inference testing, no iterative cue accumulation. For a manuscript making a clinical privacy protection claim, this is a fatal gap.
The synthetic PII injection design ensures structurally clean identity–event associations that are unrepresentative of real clinical records, where identity signals are informal, implicit, and cross-referential.

4. Editorial Decision
Reject. The novelty is insufficiently differentiated from concurrent mechanistic unlearning work, and the absence of adversarial robustness evaluation is fatal to the privacy claim. Reviewers should be asked to adjudicate: (1) whether LENS's output-tracing localisation strategy is methodologically sound given Pochinkov & Schoots's critique that such localisations perform no better than full-model updates when probed for latent knowledge; (2) whether the synthetic PII design supports the real-world generalisability claims.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Reviewers should include: (1) mechanistic interpretability in transformer-based language models, specifically causal tracing and circuit-level factual recall in decoder-only architectures; (2) machine unlearning for LLMs, with familiarity with NPO-based and gradient-ascent-based objectives and their failure modes; (3) privacy and security evaluation of language models, including membership inference attacks and adversarial prompt robustness; (4) clinical NLP and EHR text processing, with experience in MIMIC-based evaluations and the structural properties of clinical discharge narratives; (5) clinical informatics or health data governance, with awareness of GDPR, HIPAA, and post-deployment data withdrawal requirements for deployed medical AI systems.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review
The machine unlearning landscape for LLMs has advanced substantially since 2023. GA+RT and IDK+RT established foundational baselines but fail under distributional evaluation — a point this manuscript correctly leverages. The NeurIPS 2024 unlearning competition (Triantafillou et al., arXiv:2406.09073) demonstrated that most existing methods fail beyond simple token-level recall. NPO (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2025) forms the core of LENS's suppression mechanism. The LUNA method (Shen et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2502.07218) proposes neural activation redirection — structurally adjacent to LENS — and is only minimally discussed despite being a direct competitor.
The more directly competing body of work is mechanistic unlearning. Pochinkov & Schoots (arXiv:2410.12949, ICLR 2025) demonstrate that localising unlearning to mechanistically identified components produces more robust suppression, and — critically — that output-tracing localisation (i.e., activation patching recovery ratio, precisely LENS's strategy) leads to edits no better than full-model updates when probed for latent knowledge persistence. This is a direct methodological critique of LENS's localisation strategy that the manuscript does not engage with. Reviewers should be asked to adjudicate whether this invalidates the mechanistic grounding claim.

037297 prediction
Authors: Seungmin Kim, Da Eun Jeong, et al.
Corresponding Authors: Jee Yoon Park; Yeonho Choi
Date: May 2026

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript presents an AF-EV SERS-CNN platform for fetus-specific prediction of neonatal respiratory distress (RD) in twin pregnancies, claiming Step 1 AUC 0.97 (pregnancy-level screening) and Step 2 AUC 0.90 (fetal-level classification). The twin pregnancy model is biologically well-chosen: shared gestational age and maternal environment with discordant outcomes creates a natural internal control. The primary advance is domain extension — from the group's established Exosome-SERS-AI oncology work (Shin et al., Nat Commun 2023) to fetal medicine — rather than a methodological breakthrough. The Step 2 CNN trained on 26 fetuses is the single most critical vulnerability and must be adjudicated by reviewers before acceptance can be considered.

2. Strengths
Cohort partitioning at the pregnancy level rather than the spectrum or fetus level correctly prevents data leakage — a common failure in spectroscopy-AI papers. The two-step architecture is clinically coherent: Step 1 integrates paired fetal SERS spectra with maternal covariates for pregnancy-level risk, while Step 2 correctly excludes maternal variables as uninformative for within-pregnancy discrimination. The SHAP burden-stratified analysis — monotonically increasing contributions across six spectral intervals (732–1686 cm⁻¹, mean R² 96.67%) with increasing RD-affected fetuses — provides dose-response evidence that model decisions track biological signal. The clinician comparison (mean GAD-based AUC 0.80 versus Step 1 AUC 0.97) is methodologically appropriate and demonstrates a meaningful performance gap.

3. Weaknesses
The Step 2 CNN was trained on 26 fetuses (13 HC, 13 RD). This is critically underpowered for a five-block, 512-filter architecture; the training accuracy of 0.78 ± 0.008 confirms near-chance individual-spectrum performance. Calibration metrics (Brier score, calibration curves) are absent. Corresponding author Yeonho Choi holds equity in EXoPERT, and co-author Hyunku Shin is its CTO — the same company supplying the dSEC kit and SERS substrate. The manuscript does not state whether EXoPERT was involved in analysis or study design; an independent data integrity statement is required. The outcome definition — any oxygen support within 24 hours, including HFNC — conflates transient post-cesarean adaptation with true RD; confirmed RDS occurred in only 3% of neonates. Model performance stratified by severity is absent. 1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript presents SkinGPT-X, a multimodal multi-agent system integrating a Vision Agent (Qwen3-VL), a Pre-Diagnosis Agent (fine-tuned PanDerm), a RAG module anchored to the Oxford Handbook of Medical Dermatology, and EvoDerma-Mem — a self-evolving memory that iteratively synthesizes diagnostic guidelines from accumulating cases. Evaluation spans four standard benchmarks, a reconstructed high-cardinality dataset (DermNet498, 498 classes), and a curated rare disease suite (RSDD, n=564, 8 conditions). The central claim occupies genuine white space in the field.
The two governing concerns are the near-complete absence of prospective or external clinical validation, and the RSDD benchmark's critically small per-class sample sizes with no reported confidence intervals.

2. Strengths
EvoDerma-Mem is the paper's most distinctive contribution. The closed-loop guideline evolution formalism (Equations 8–9) and ablation on Fitzpatrick-17k showing +4.2% Weighted F1 attributable specifically to memory inclusion (P < 0.001) provide quantitative credibility to the design. DermNet498's reorganisation of 23 super-classes into 498 clinically grounded sub-classes is a substantive benchmark contribution that creates a harder, more realistic evaluation target than existing public datasets. The blinded physician validation (n=100 cases, two senior dermatologists) yielding scores above 4.88/5 across three guideline quality dimensions is supportive, though inter-rater agreement is not reported. Inclusion of DDI31 and Fitzpatrick-17k appropriately addresses known demographic bias concerns in dermatological AI.

3. Weaknesses
The RSDD is statistically fragile: 564 samples across 8 classes with a 1:2 train-test split yields fewer than 8 training images for Cryopyrin-associated periodic syndrome (n=22 total). Per-class confidence intervals are absent. The claimed +9.8% ACC improvement likely reflects performance on the three largest classes rather than genuine few-shot generalisation. All evaluation is retrospective on curated or web-scraped datasets; no prospective clinical cohort is included. A May 2025 multi-site evaluation of leading dermatological MLLMs across 5,811 real-world cases demonstrated a substantial benchmark-to-bedside performance gap — a directly contradicting finding the authors do not address. No disaggregated Fitzpatrick-type performance tables are provided despite the fairness claims. Inference latency for the five-agent sequential pipeline is unquantified.

4. Editorial Decision
Send for Review. EvoDerma-Mem and DermNet498 are sufficiently novel to merit external peer assessment. Reviewers must adjudicate: (1) whether RSDD sample sizes support the few-shot generalisation claim; (2) whether two-rater physician validation without inter-rater reliability meets clinical AI standards; and (3) whether the system is computationally feasible in real dermatology workflows.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review (Past 3 Years)
The field of AI-driven posterior eye shape quantification has developed along two parallel tracks since 2022. The first is OCT-based shape reconstruction: PESNet (Zhang et al., MICCAI 2023) proposed a dual-branch network for 3D posterior shape reconstruction from small-FOV OCT using a Polar Voxelization Block, but remained confined to OCT inputs and limited fields of view. The second track, monocular depth estimation from fundus images, has been largely characterized by narrow-region approaches that use depth as an auxiliary feature for segmentation tasks rather than full-field PES reconstruction — as reviewed in Yii et al. (Ophthalmic Physiol Opt., 2025), who demonstrated on UK Biobank data that fundus features do encode eye shape information beyond spherical equivalent, but did not attempt metric 3D reconstruction. In pathologic myopia detection, direct AI models applied to fundus photographs have achieved pooled AUCs of ~0.99 for PM detection in a meta-analysis (Zhang et al., Eye, 2023), which substantially exceeds the AUC of 0.95 reported for Depth-Retina's PES index vector in cross-sectional PM detection — a comparison the authors do not engage with. This is a critical omission: the manuscript must justify why an indirect PES-mediated approach is clinically preferable to direct fundus-based PM classification if the latter already achieves near-perfect discrimination. The 10-year prospective prediction result (AUC 0.90) is more defensible on novelty grounds, as no direct fundus AI model has been validated for this task at 10-year horizon in a population-based cohort. Foundation models for general ophthalmic imaging (e.g., the multimodal vision-language model reported in npj Digital Medicine, 2025) are now reaching clinical breadth that will set a high bar for disease-specific contributions; the authors' claim that PES provides additive value over CFP appearance must be tested directly, not assumed.
