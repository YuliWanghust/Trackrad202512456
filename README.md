### 1. Overall Assessment

This manuscript proposes a Bayesian transfer learning framework for augmenting RCT control arms using synthetic "digital control" (DC) samples generated by a covariate generative model (G_ϕ, Bayesian network) and an outcome predictive model (f_θ, BART) trained on RWD, with adaptive integration via a spike-and-slab hierarchical prior. Theoretical guarantees cover consistency, asymptotic normality, type I error control, and power. Two oncology case studies using Project Data Sphere data are included.

The framework is internally coherent and the theoretical apparatus is non-trivial. However, the robustness claims rest on a confounding model that operationalizes unmeasured confounding as a scalar intercept shift Δ — a perturbation detectable by the marginal residual statistic δ_{f_θ} and therefore structurally favorable to the proposed method. Conditional confounding, where bias does not shift the marginal mean, is never evaluated. Additionally, no joint (X, Y) fidelity metric is applied to the DC pipeline, leaving quality assessment dependent on marginal statistics that cannot detect outcome surface misspecification.

---

### 2. Strengths

Theorems 1–3 and Corollary 1 collectively establish consistency, asymptotic efficiency, type I error control, and power superiority — a scope of formal guarantees uncommon in this literature, where SAM (Yang et al., 2023) and commensurate prior methods offer no comparable theoretical coverage.

The decentralized training architecture — sharing only f_θ model weights rather than patient-level RWD — is a structurally enforced privacy mechanism absent from all comparator methods, with direct relevance to GDPR- and HIPAA-constrained multi-institutional settings.

Simulations span six covariate distribution scenarios with 10,000 replicates, supplemented by ablation over borrowing strategies and sensitivity analyses over n_dc and K, substantively exceeding methodological norms in this literature.

The two Project Data Sphere applications demonstrate plausible adaptive shrinkage: DC achieves the smallest bias among data integration methods in both pancreatic and colorectal cancer settings under conditions where PS-based comparators deviate materially.

---

### 3. Weaknesses

The confounding robustness claim rests on a uniform intercept shift Δ with identical covariate distributions — a one-dimensional perturbation that is marginally detectable. Realistic confounding acts conditionally through effect modification or differential inclusion criteria and may not shift the marginal mean while still biasing the ATE. No simulation scenario tests conditional confounding with Δ = 0 in expectation, which is the setting the framework most needs to survive.

The DC quality metrics — marginal residuals δ_{f_θ} and energy distance δ_{G_ϕ} — evaluate covariates and outcomes in isolation. No metric assesses joint (X, Y) fidelity. A generative model reproducing covariate marginals while operating on a shifted outcome surface would receive high δ_{G_ϕ} and evade detection unless the outcome shift is marginally visible — a scenario consistent with the known failure modes of BART-based imputation under covariate shift.

Real-data evaluations lack independent external validation. The colorectal application uses a within-RCT cross-validation split that conflates estimation and validation; the pancreatic application provides no interval calibration. Neither constitutes externally held-out validation, and neither tests the framework under the conditional confounding conditions that motivate it.

---

### 4. Editorial Decision

**Reject.** The marginal confounding benchmark is not a credible stress test for a method whose central claim is robustness to unmeasured confounders. The absence of joint (X, Y) fidelity validation is a structural gap in the validity argument, not a presentation limitation. Taken together, the robustness claims in Sections 2.1 and 3 are not supported by evidence that would withstand specialist scrutiny. The work would be more appropriate for *Biometrics*, *Statistics in Medicine*, or *Journal of Biopharmaceutical Statistics*, where the methodological contribution can be evaluated on its own terms without the clinical impact bar required here.

### 6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review

The field of external control augmentation for RCTs has advanced substantially in the past three years along two converging tracks. On the Bayesian adaptive borrowing track, the SAM prior (Yang et al., 2023, *Biometrics* 79:2857–2868) and its propensity-score-integrated extension PS-SAM (Zhao et al., 2025, *J Biopharm Stat*) represent the current state of the art for adaptive mixture priors that respond to RWD–RCT outcome heterogeneity while retaining valid type I error in the presence of unmeasured confounders. The PW-MEM method (Wei et al., 2024, *Stat Med* 43:3815–3829) combines propensity-score weighting with multi-source exchangeability modelling to assess outcome distributional consistency after covariate balancing. Baron et al. (2025, *Stat Biosci* 17:683–708) extend the power prior via a divide-and-conquer stratification strategy with plausibility indices. On the generative synthetic control track, a VAE-based approach for survival endpoints (arXiv:2511.16551, 2024) and Bøe et al. (*PLOS Digital Health*, 2025) have addressed synthetic external controls under privacy constraints, finding systematic miscalibration of type I error and power that requires post-generation selection procedures — a concern directly relevant to the DC framework. Qiu et al. (arXiv:2501.17835, 2025) propose A-TMLE for RCT augmentation with outcome-blind matching, providing a semiparametric competitor the authors do not benchmark against.

This manuscript occupies a distinctive position in the field: it is the first to combine a generative model for covariate synthesis, a predictive model for outcome imputation, and a Bayesian hierarchical prior for adaptive integration into a single unified framework with formal guarantees. This distinguishes it from PS-SAM and PW-MEM, which operate on observed RWD rather than generated data. The key gap, however, is that the field's recent work on generative synthetic controls has identified joint (X, Y) fidelity and calibrated type I error as the primary failure modes of generative approaches — and the DC framework does not yet address these adequately. The authors' use of BART as f_θ without a joint density formulation, and the reliance on marginal residuals for quality assessment, leaves the framework exposed to the same miscalibration risks identified in recent generative control arm literature.

### Additional Literature Review: Papers Sharing Similar Scope (Past 3 Years)

**1. Zhao Y. et al. "PS-SAM: Propensity-score-integrated self-adapting mixture prior to dynamically and efficiently borrow information from historical data." *J Biopharm Stat* (2025).**
Combines propensity score adjustment with a SAM mixture prior to enable adaptive borrowing under both measured and unmeasured confounding. The adaptive weight is determined by posterior consistency between PS-adjusted external and internal control outcomes. Directly comparable to the DC framework: both address unmeasured confounding and adaptive down-weighting, but PS-SAM operates on observed RWD rather than generated surrogates and requires individual-level data sharing, which the DC framework avoids.

**2. Wei W. et al. "Propensity score weighted multi-source exchangeability models for incorporating external control data in randomized clinical trials." *Stat Med* 43:3815–3829 (2024).**
PW-MEM integrates propensity-score weighting with multi-source exchangeability modelling to evaluate outcome distributional similarity post-balancing, borrowing information proportionally. Applied to Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative data. A direct comparator already included in the DC simulation benchmarks; the manuscript demonstrates DC outperforms PW-MEM in scenarios with covariate mean/variance shift, but PW-MEM achieves competitive efficiency under perfectly consistent RWD.

**3. Baron E. et al. "Enhancing Randomized Controlled Trials: A Bayesian Divide-and-Conquer Approach for Borrowing External Control Data." *Stat Biosci* 17:683–708 (2025).**
Proposes stratified Bayesian borrowing using plausibility indices derived from stratum-specific power priors, extending the borrowing-by-parts framework to handle temporal effects and stratum-level heterogeneity. Like the DC framework, it quantifies borrowing quality locally rather than globally. However, it remains tied to observed RWD and does not address privacy, placing it in a complementary rather than competing category.

**4. Qiu S. et al. "An Estimator-Robust Design for Augmenting Randomized Controlled Trials with External Real-World Data." arXiv:2501.17835 (2025).**
Applies adaptive TMLE (A-TMLE) to decompose the ATE into a pooled estimand and a bias-correction estimand, with outcome-blind matching on trial enrollment and propensity scores. Demonstrated on the DEVOTE cardiovascular trial augmented by Optum Clinformatics. A semiparametric competitor not benchmarked in the DC paper; its doubly-robust construction provides type I error guarantees under weaker model assumptions than the DC framework's correct-specification requirement.

**5. Annoni M. et al. "Toward Valid Generative Clinical Trial Data with Survival Endpoints." arXiv:2511.16551 (2024).**
Proposes a VAE for joint generation of mixed-type covariates and survival outcomes under censoring, evaluating synthetic controls for both privacy-constrained data sharing and control arm augmentation. Identifies systematic type I error miscalibration and power distortion in generative control arm settings, requiring a post-generation selection step. Directly relevant to the DC framework's reliance on BART-generated outcomes without survival extension and without a joint fidelity validation step.

**6. Bøe S. et al. "Synthetic data as external control arms in scarce single-arm clinical trials." *PLOS Digital Health* (2025).**
Evaluates multiple synthetic data generation techniques — including CTGAN, TVAE, and PrivBayes — as external control arms for single-arm oncology studies using Norwegian health registry data, under both utility and privacy metrics. Finds that no single generator dominates across fidelity and privacy criteria, and that the choice of synthesis method materially affects downstream inferential validity. Highlights the model-selection problem that the DC framework implicitly faces when choosing between BN, GAN, ARF, and TVAE for G_ϕ.
