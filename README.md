036325 Framework

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript introduces ClimAID, a Python-based framework integrating CMIP6 climate projections, lagged meteorological predictors, stacked machine learning, isotonic calibration, and an LLM-assisted reporting module into a pipeline for district-level climate-sensitive infectious disease modelling. It is demonstrated on dengue in a single district in Pune, Maharashtra, spanning 2012–2023.
The manuscript reads primarily as a software descriptor rather than an empirical scientific advance. Single-district, single-disease validation provides insufficient evidence of generalisation. R² of 0.67–0.77 is reported across configuration modes without uncertainty quantification or comparison to any epidemiological baseline. The LLM reporting module raises unresolved hallucination and clinical reliability concerns the authors do not address.

2. Strengths
The deterministic pipeline design is a genuine contribution. Fixing random seeds across Optuna's TPE sampler, joblib parallelisation, and model-specific training ensures cross-environment reproducibility — directly addressing documented stochasticity challenges in ML-based epidemiological modelling.
The three-stage stacked architecture — base model, residual correction, isotonic calibration — is well-motivated for overdispersed, non-stationary dengue count data. Isotonic regression enforces monotonic consistency between predicted and observed values, reducing systematic bias during high-variability epidemic periods.
The dual-baseline outbreak risk framework, combining a static historical quantile threshold with a dynamic CMIP6-derived window-specific threshold aggregated across GCM ensembles, provides a principled probabilistic approach to communicating uncertainty for public health decision-making.

3. Weaknesses
Validation is restricted to a single district. The claim that ClimAID supports global application is not supported by any result. There is no cross-district, cross-disease, or cross-country validation. Multi-site generalisability is the minimum standard in this field, as demonstrated by Sebastianelli et al. (Sci. Rep., 2024) across Brazil and Peru.
There is no comparison against standard epidemiological baselines. RMSE of 14.34–15.42 and R² of 0.67–0.77 are reported in isolation. A DLNM, SARIMA, or negative binomial regression comparator is absent, making it impossible to assess whether the stacked ML architecture provides meaningful gains over well-established methods.
The LLM reporting module contains no evaluation of output accuracy, consistency, or fidelity. Automated policy-oriented health summaries generated by Phi-3 or Mistral from climate projections, without any reliability assessment, represent a patient safety concern that the authors dismiss by noting the LLM does not affect model training — which is not the relevant risk.

4. Editorial Decision
Desk Reject. Single-district validation, absent baselines, missing uncertainty quantification, and an unevaluated LLM reporting module are not addressable through minor revision. The authors should resubmit following multi-district or multi-disease validation, inclusion of DLNM or comparable baselines, and formal evaluation of LLM output reliability. In its current form, the work is better suited to Journal of Open Source Software or SoftwareX.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Reviewers should include experts in the following areas: (1) Bayesian and ensemble machine learning for non-stationary ecological count data, with specific experience in AutoML optimisation frameworks such as Optuna or FLAML; (2) distributed lag non-linear modelling and time-series epidemiology for vector-borne diseases, particularly dengue in tropical settings; (3) CMIP6 downscaling and bias-correction methods for regional climate impact assessments, with awareness of TPQM and quantile mapping artefacts; (4) public health early warning systems for climate-sensitive diseases in South or Southeast Asia, with operational deployment experience; (5) evaluation of LLM reliability in clinical or public health decision support contexts.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review (Past 3 Years)
The climate-disease modelling field has advanced substantially since 2023. Sebastianelli et al. (Sci. Rep., 2024) demonstrated a reproducible ensemble ML framework for dengue forecasting across Brazil's 27 federal units, with explicit transferability testing in Peru — a multi-site standard that ClimAID does not approach. Phung et al. (Nat. Commun., 2025) addressed the adoptability and sustainability of digital prediction tools for climate-sensitive infectious diseases, highlighting that operational deployment requires demonstrated robustness across heterogeneous surveillance contexts, not single-site proof-of-concept. A major 2025 Nature review on AI for infectious disease modelling emphasised explainability, safety, and accountability as core requirements — dimensions ClimAID's LLM module does not adequately address. Rocklöv et al. (Lancet Reg. Health Europe, 2023) provided a comprehensive framework for decision-support tools for climate-resilient infectious disease management in Europe, establishing a benchmark for what multi-disease, multi-site, policy-oriented frameworks must demonstrate. ClimAID does not cite or engage with Phung et al. (2025) or Rocklöv et al. (2023), both of which are directly competing frameworks. The manuscript's positioning as a novel integration of CMIP6 projections with ML calibration is weakened by the existence of prior work combining these elements, including Colón-González et al. and Baker et al. 035599 Images

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript introduces TEAMKidney, a two-stage deep learning framework combining HRNet-based semantic segmentation with a custom Mask2Former-derived panoptic model (Glom2Mask) for GBM and PFP morphometry across mouse, rat, and human TEM images. The central claim is expert-level measurement accuracy substantially outperforming FPW-DL.
The work is clinically motivated and technically competent. However, its novelty is materially undermined by TEM-AID (Ma et al., JAMA Network Open, October 2025), a multicenter system trained on 160,727 images from 31,670 patients across six centers that performs segmentation, morphometry, and disease subtype classification. The second critical concern is the validation cohort of only 246 images across 25 glomeruli — insufficient to substantiate cross-species generalizability claims.

2. Strengths
The multi-species, multi-disease training dataset spanning human DKD, Fabry disease, and mouse and rat injury models across 12,991 images exceeds most prior single-species studies and directly addresses FPW-DL's known failure on rodent tissue. Patient-level stratification at an 80/20 split is methodologically sound.
Glom2Mask's panoptic formulation — classifying GBM as "stuff" and PFP as "things" — directly addresses the fundamental limitation of semantic methods that cannot individuate foot process instances. Outperforming Mask2Former at 66,000 versus 360,000 training iterations is a concrete, reproducible finding.
The FPW-DL comparison is rigorous and mechanistically informative. Identifying slit diaphragm proxy detection as the root cause of FPW-DL's bias, and reducing mean PFP width error from 24.80 to 4.46 pixels, substantiates TEAMKidney's design rationale.

3. Weaknesses
The ground-truth validation dataset is critically underpowered. Twenty-five glomeruli across three species cannot substantiate cross-species generalizability at this journal's standard. Confidence intervals are absent from most comparisons, and null hypothesis retention with small N is not equivalent to demonstrated equivalence.
TEM-AID (Ma et al., JAMA Network Open, 2025) is unacknowledged. Trained on 160,727 images from 31,670 patients across six centers with external validation AUC of 0.972–0.989, it directly contests TEAMKidney's novelty claims. This omission is not acceptable.
The self-training contribution is unvalidated. No ablation isolates pseudo-label benefit from architectural and dataset effects. Confidence thresholds and error propagation risk are unreported.
The DKD cohort lacks demographic and clinical staging detail. The authors' own unexpected finding of modest PFP preservation in DKD may reflect cohort composition rather than biology, yet no patient-level metadata is provided to adjudicate this.

4. Editorial Decision
Reject; transfer recommended to npj Digital Medicine or Kidney International. The panoptic architecture is technically motivated and the multi-species scope is a genuine contribution, but two problems are unresolvable without major new work: the validation dataset is too small, and the failure to engage with TEM-AID misrepresents the state of the field. Resubmission requires external validation on a minimum of 500 glomeruli from multiple institutions, a full self-training ablation, and head-to-head comparison with TEM-AID. Reviewers should adjudicate whether Glom2Mask's panoptic design offers sufficient architectural novelty over TEM-AID's YOLO-v8 + SAM approach, and whether cross-species advantage over FPW-DL holds under matched resolution conditions.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Reviewers should collectively cover: (1) panoptic and instance segmentation architectures, specifically transformer-based models such as Mask2Former and HRNet applied to dense biological image analysis; (2) semi-supervised and self-training methods for medical image segmentation with limited annotation budgets; (3) computational renal pathology and TEM-based morphometry, with direct experience annotating or interpreting GBM and PFP ultrastructure; (4) clinical nephrology and glomerular disease diagnosis, specifically DKD, Fabry disease, and Alport syndrome, with familiarity with TEM-based clinical reporting standards; and (5) statistical methodology for method comparison studies, including equivalence testing, Bland-Altman analysis, and confidence interval reporting in morphometric studies.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review
The TEM image analysis field has advanced considerably since 2023. The dominant shift has been from U-Net semantic segmentation of single structures toward integrated multi-task systems. FPW-DL (Zimmermann et al.) established the open-source benchmark for PFP width quantification from human TEM data. The GBMSeg framework (arxiv:2406.16271, 2024) introduced one-shot, reference-guided, training-free prompt engineering for GBM segmentation using SAM, directly contesting the claim that specialized fine-tuning is necessary. Renal-AI (2025) applied YOLOv8-OBB for multi-class detection across six ultrastructural features including mesangial deposits and subepithelial deposits — two classes entirely absent from TEAMKidney. Most consequentially, TEM-AID (Ma et al., JAMA Network Open, October 2025) trained on 160,727 images from 31,670 patients across six centers integrates YOLO-v8 detection, SAM-based segmentation, and disease subtype classification, achieving external validation AUC of 0.972–0.989 across five independent test sets. Against this landscape, TEAMKidney's primary differentiators are its cross-species scope (mouse, rat, human), its panoptic rather than detection-based treatment of PFP instances, and its open-source commitment. These are meaningful, but the manuscript does not argue them forcefully or comparatively. The authors must reframe their novelty around these specific advantages rather than claiming primacy they no longer hold. nih

035748 Support

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript presents FusionPredictor, a multimodal LLM-based framework combining structured EHR data with unstructured clinical text for sepsis screening and in-hospital mortality prediction, augmented by Integrated Gradients and RAG-based explanation. The central claim is simultaneous competitive predictive performance and clinically grounded interpretability, validated through a simulated ICU deployment across 200 cases.
The fundamental problem is that the paper conflates system description with scientific validation. The simulated clinical evaluation — five intensivists rating 200 cases using custom Likert-scale metrics — functions as anecdotal rather than controlled evidence. These issues are not addressable through revision alone.

2. Strengths
The multimodal fusion architecture is technically coherent. Projecting structured clinical variables into the LLM's embedding space via representations extracted from the final five hidden layers, then integrating through mean pooling, is a principled approach that avoids naive feature concatenation common in earlier sepsis prediction work.
External validation across three geographically distinct cohorts — West China Shangjin Nanfu, West China Tianfu, and MIMIC-IV — is a genuine strength, with ROC-AUC values of 0.806–0.875 for screening and 0.777–0.869 for mortality, demonstrating non-trivial generalizability across institutional contexts.
Grounding LLM explanations in Integrated Gradients token-level attribution rather than purely generative outputs represents a meaningful methodological step toward faithful interpretability, distinguishing this work from systems producing post-hoc explanations without formal attribution.

3. Weaknesses
The clinical evaluation is fundamentally underpowered. Five intensivists evaluating 200 cases in shadow mode — without randomisation, blinding, or a pre-registered protocol — cannot substantiate claims that the agent outperformed general-purpose LLMs. Inter-rater reliability, rater calibration, and score threshold justification are entirely absent.
Interpretability is claimed but not validated. Attributing clinical meaning to NRBC and serum calcium without systematic comparison to Sepsis-3 criteria, clinician-grounded relevance assessment, or attribution stability testing leaves these claims illustrative rather than evidential.
Calibration metrics are absent throughout. Brier score, ECE, and reliability diagrams are standard requirements for any paper claiming clinical utility; their omission is not acceptable. Confidence intervals are similarly unreported across all performance tables.

4. Editorial Decision
Reject. The clinical evaluation rests on an uncontrolled five-clinician simulation that does not constitute evidence of clinical utility at Nature Communications standard. Interpretability claims are asserted rather than validated, and the absence of calibration and confidence intervals means the performance tables cannot be critically evaluated. These are not revision-addressable. If sent for review, reviewers should adjudicate: (1) whether attributions faithfully reflect model reasoning versus spurious correlations, (2) whether the 200-case simulation constitutes valid comparative evidence, and (3) whether label leakage from clinical text to ICD-coded outcomes has been ruled out.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Reviewers should include experts in: (1) gradient-based attribution methods for transformer models, specifically Integrated Gradients applied to clinical sequence data; (2) LLM agent architectures and RAG system evaluation, including RAGAS and related faithfulness metrics; (3) multimodal EHR modelling combining structured tabular data and free-text clinical narratives; (4) sepsis phenotyping, Sepsis-3 criteria, and ICU prognostication; and (5) human factors and controlled clinical evaluation design for AI decision-support tools, including blinding methodology and inter-rater reliability assessment.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review
The past three years have seen substantial convergence on LLM-based clinical decision support for sepsis and critical care. The COMPOSER-LLM system (medrxiv, 2025) prospectively validated a multimodal architecture combining structured EHR data with clinical notes for sepsis differential diagnosis — a standard the present manuscript does not meet. Multi-agent RAG systems for sepsis management have been demonstrated with guideline compliance validation and programmatic grounding metrics. More broadly, LLM-agent frameworks for clinical triage and diagnosis on MIMIC-IV have been rigorously benchmarked using multiple model families with structured evaluation protocols (npj Digital Medicine, 2025). The interpretability literature has moved toward faithfulness-first evaluation: attribution methods are now expected to be validated against clinician ground truth, not illustrated with cherry-picked examples.
Against this landscape, the manuscript's predictive performance (ROC-AUC 0.818–0.896) does not exceed XGBoost and LightGBM benchmarks on MIMIC-IV (0.880–0.912, as the authors acknowledge), and the agent's advantage over general-purpose LLMs rests solely on the underpowered clinician evaluation. The paper does not engage with COMPOSER-LLM, the multi-agent sepsis management literature, or contemporary faithfulness evaluation frameworks. These omissions materially weaken the novelty claim.

035500 clinical utility

1. Overall Assessment
This manuscript presents Depth-Retina, a deep learning framework estimating physically calibrated, full-field 3D posterior eye shape (PES) from a single CFP or UWF-CFP. The central claim is that a dual-branch ViT encoder with depthwise large-kernel refinement and a ZoeDepth-style metric head reconstructs Bruch's membrane geometry over a 20 × 20 mm posterior pole at spatial RMSE of 0.070 mm, and that reconstructed PES outperforms refractive error and axial length for cross-sectional PM detection (AUC 0.95 vs. 0.82/0.77) and 10-year incident PM prediction (AUC 0.90 vs. 0.78). The problem is clinically motivated and the technical approach is coherent. Two concerns dominate: the training and all downstream validation cohorts are overwhelmingly Han Chinese and Mongolian, and the glaucoma and AMD analyses are exploratory black-box classifications with no confidence intervals and no CFP-based comparator.
2. Strengths
The dual-branch ViT encoder — processing both native CFP and a 2× super-resolved input — is a targeted solution to texture-to-depth transfer failure in general-purpose MDE. The ablation in Fig. 4C quantifies the architectural contribution independently of metric supervision: fine-tuned ZoeDepth achieves δ₁ = 0.817 versus Depth-Retina's 0.931 internally, with a larger gap externally (0.588 vs. 0.918). The ground-truth pipeline — 27,648 UWF-OCT B-scans, SLO-based co-registration, raster OCT verification at RMSE 0.037 ± 0.008 mm — is rigorous. The 10-year prospective PM prediction (AUC 0.90) in a 726-eye Beijing Eye Study cohort with 155 incident cases and DeLong-tested AUC comparison is the most defensible and novel clinical result.
3. Weaknesses
All validation cohorts are ethnically homogeneous. PM morphology and PES norms are population-specific; the generalizability claim is overstated without a non-East-Asian validation cohort. The glaucoma and AMD analyses use SE-ResNet applied to reconstructed PES images, with no morphometric features, no interpretability, no confidence intervals, and no direct comparison against fundus-based AI classifiers on the same data — methodologically inconsistent with the PM analysis and insufficient to support claims of cross-disease clinical utility. The PES index vector construction and Gaussian mixture model are deferred entirely to Supplementary Appendix 4; prospective feature definition and overfitting risk cannot be assessed from the main text. OCT ground-truth varies across three different vendor systems and field-of-views (6–20 mm) in external sets; absolute geometric accuracy is not established with cross-vendor calibration.
4. Editorial Decision
Send for Review, with major reservations. The technical contribution and ground-truth construction are sufficiently novel for external evaluation. Reviewers must adjudicate: (1) whether the PM, glaucoma, and AMD clinical analyses are methodologically sound given their asymmetry; (2) whether absence of confidence intervals and CFP-based comparators for glaucoma and AMD AUCs is a fatal flaw; and (3) whether ethnic homogeneity limits clinical claims to the point requiring non-East-Asian external validation before acceptance.

5. Suggested Reviewer Expertise
Reviewers should collectively cover: (1) metric monocular depth estimation and ViT-based encoder-decoder architectures, with specific experience in domain adaptation from general-scene to medical imaging; (2) OCT-based 3D surface reconstruction, multimodal co-registration of fundus and volumetric OCT, and ground-truth construction for retinal geometry; (3) clinical myopia research, specifically pathologic myopia epidemiology and biomarker validation in longitudinal cohorts; and (4) statistical methodology for diagnostic and prognostic AI validation in ophthalmology, including AUC comparison methods, calibration analysis, and subgroup fairness assessment.

6. State-of-the-Art Literature Review (Past 3 Years)
The field of AI-driven posterior eye shape quantification has developed along two parallel tracks since 2022. The first is OCT-based shape reconstruction: PESNet (Zhang et al., MICCAI 2023) proposed a dual-branch network for 3D posterior shape reconstruction from small-FOV OCT using a Polar Voxelization Block, but remained confined to OCT inputs and limited fields of view. The second track, monocular depth estimation from fundus images, has been largely characterized by narrow-region approaches that use depth as an auxiliary feature for segmentation tasks rather than full-field PES reconstruction — as reviewed in Yii et al. (Ophthalmic Physiol Opt., 2025), who demonstrated on UK Biobank data that fundus features do encode eye shape information beyond spherical equivalent, but did not attempt metric 3D reconstruction. In pathologic myopia detection, direct AI models applied to fundus photographs have achieved pooled AUCs of ~0.99 for PM detection in a meta-analysis (Zhang et al., Eye, 2023), which substantially exceeds the AUC of 0.95 reported for Depth-Retina's PES index vector in cross-sectional PM detection — a comparison the authors do not engage with. This is a critical omission: the manuscript must justify why an indirect PES-mediated approach is clinically preferable to direct fundus-based PM classification if the latter already achieves near-perfect discrimination. The 10-year prospective prediction result (AUC 0.90) is more defensible on novelty grounds, as no direct fundus AI model has been validated for this task at 10-year horizon in a population-based cohort. Foundation models for general ophthalmic imaging (e.g., the multimodal vision-language model reported in npj Digital Medicine, 2025) are now reaching clinical breadth that will set a high bar for disease-specific contributions; the authors' claim that PES provides additive value over CFP appearance must be tested directly, not assumed.
