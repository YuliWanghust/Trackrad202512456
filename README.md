Thanks, Ibukun — agree the sample size and multi-country DHS harmonisation are the strongest points in its favour, but I don't think that offsets the validation picture. The "partial" external validation is really the paper's only true generalizability test (Pakistan withheld entirely, GRU trained on the other five), and it isn't partial — it fails outright (mean −32.9% vs. no-change). The within-country result they lead with (5/6 countries beat no-change) is also not significant at n=6 (p=0.109), so the two validation claims that would justify sending this out are, respectively, negative and non-significant.

I'd also flag that the 14 variables are sociodemographic/household infrastructure, not health outcomes — the authors say so themselves in Methods — yet the title and abstract frame this as "population health" surveillance throughout. That's a construct problem independent of the validation issue.

My recommendation is Reject, with a pointer to npj Digital Medicine — the design-consistent survey-weighted architecture is a genuine contribution and could clear the bar there once rescoped around what's actually validated (within-country structural forecasting) rather than the cross-country claim that failed. **Reviewer 1 (Yang, Professor, China)** — Central objection is circularity: the patient simulator and BeTA are trained/evaluated on the same four-way taxonomy (Normal/Chaos/Concealing/Both), so the evaluation environment is defined by BeTA's own modeling assumptions. He notes the simulator runs on a weaker backbone (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) than BeTA (Qwen3-8B), and that the automated fidelity metrics (CSF/DSA/CTA/HFLR/HFR/RCS) only confirm instruction compliance, not resemblance to real patients. He flags that the taxonomy's own validity is unsupported (κ=0.72 measures transcript quality, not taxonomy validity) and raises what he calls the "Oracle paradox": C5-Oracle using the ground-truth type label (0.419) scores *below* BeTA using its own inferred belief (0.426) — evidence the discrete taxonomy carries little information, undermining its rationale. He also flags a safety concern: turn-efficiency as an optimization target may incentivize premature diagnostic closure. He recommends specific fixes (cross-validation against a stronger/heterogeneous simulator, comparison to real consultation transcripts) rather than declaring the paper unfixable.

**Reviewer 2 (Zhang, ECR/Lecturer, China)** — Co-reviewed with Reviewer 1 as part of an ECR mentorship pairing; no independent substantive concerns are recorded. Confirms code is documented and reproducible.

**Reviewer 3 (Holderried, Lecturer, Germany)** — Recommends Major Revision. Most damaging point: "Diag-Acc," the paper's central endpoint, is ROUGE-L/BERTScore-F1 text similarity against a reference diagnosis, not a clinically adjudicated correctness measure — yet it is called "diagnostic accuracy" throughout the abstract, results, and discussion, and the model is *trained* on this reward, biasing it relative to untrained baselines. She also shows the "emergent" belief-to-strategy mappings are partly hardcoded (Q2 for Chaos, Q4 for Concealing are predetermined in training), contradicting the manuscript's framing of these as learned. She identifies an unfair comparison: BeTA receives a disease-specific dimension list derived from the ground-truth knowledge graph, while frontier baselines are evaluated zero-shot without this scaffolding. Statistically, she notes repeated-measures/paired data (same cases across systems, same physicians rating all episodes) are analyzed with methods assuming independence (two-way ANOVA, Kruskal–Wallis) rather than mixed models or Friedman tests. She flags that clinical claims ("clinically validated," "confirmed generalizability," "clinically faithful") are unsupported since all evaluation occurs inside the simulated environment with no real-patient data. Minor: only 10% of translated episodes were clinically reviewed, model-naming mismatches between manuscript and repository (Qwen2.5-7B vs Qwen3-4B), disease-count mismatches (973 vs 936), and a request to confirm that published transcript data don't contain real patient/physician names.

**Decision: Major Revision.**

No reviewer alleges fabrication, and both substantive reviewers frame their objections as addressable — R1 names the specific validation experiments needed (heterogeneous/stronger simulator, real-transcript comparison); R3 explicitly recommends major revision and specifies what's required (blinded clinical adjudication of diagnoses, corrected statistics, honest labeling of predetermined vs. emergent components, toned-down clinical claims). That satisfies the bar for revision rather than reject.

That said, this is a close call, and the counterargument for outright reject is real: the circularity Reviewer 1 identifies — same taxonomy defining the simulator, the training environment, and the evaluation — is exactly the "training environment = evaluation environment" failure mode that is often terminal for RL/agent papers, because it can inflate every downstream number simultaneously rather than in one isolated metric. Combined with Reviewer 3's finding that the primary endpoint is mislabeled (text similarity presented as diagnostic accuracy) and that "emergent" behavior is partly scripted, the cumulative effect is that most of the paper's headline claims (44% turn reduction, 71.5% preference win rate, "clinically faithful") rest on a self-referential benchmark. If revision does not produce (a) performance on a heterogeneous/independently-validated simulator or real transcripts, and (b) a genuinely clinically-adjudicated diagnostic accuracy measure, the paper reduces to a well-executed but self-validating RL exercise — at which point the decision should revert to Reject or transfer (npj Digital Medicine is the natural landing spot given the clinical dialogue focus).

**Tripwire for the revision:** if diagnostic performance collapses when scored against physician-adjudicated ground truth rather than ROUGE/BERTScore, or if BeTA's advantage disappears on an out-of-framework/stronger simulator, this reverts to Reject.
